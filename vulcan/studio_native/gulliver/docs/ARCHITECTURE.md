# Architecture

`assembly_guide.py` reads a kept build's `report.json`, `params.json`, thumbnail and original binary STL files. The map unions their coplanar lowest faces and fills engraving holes, retaining concave cut edges; it is the base footprint, not a projection of overhangs or a drilling template. Pieces keep their file IDs and stable guide numbers, and move apart for legibility; framed panels receive space for their separate frames. No current profile/settings are used. Missing legacy snapshots leave pin/label metadata unknown. `model_assembly` returns paged file/material data; `model_assembly_export` writes only `assembly-{es,en}.html` and `assembly-map.svg` beside the originals. Ordinary files/export discovers these optional artifacts without changing report bytes. HTML embeds the SVG and thumbnail and has print styles. `AssemblyGuide.jsx` follows the current/history selection in the model viewer, including per-build download links.

Printing profiles: `printing.py` validates profiles and atomically stores `print-profiles.json`. `ModelParams.print_profile` is the immutable model/build snapshot. Services and workers resolve its bed consistently; explicit selection sets the nozzle while later manual edits remain possible. `surface_mm2` plus solid volume feed the shell/infill estimate; per-part volume shares receive density/price/colour overrides. Missing prices remain unknown. `model_materials` is a pure quote of existing geometry. New builds export profile colours through the ordinary 3MF/GLB/thumb path.

Printability locations use Z-up preview millimetres and per-part IDs. Viewer3D adds disposable instanced rings to their tile groups, so exploded tiles carry the markers; these nodes never enter a mesh/export. Legacy reports without surface/location data retain their old viewer and offer regeneration. `model_issues` returns stable pages with total/offset/next_offset so the shared response budget cannot hide the remaining coordinates. UI, REST and MCP share six typed handlers for profiles, applying a profile, material estimates and issue locations.

`sources/dtm.py` selects only verified bare-earth WCS/WMS layers for regional Spain, AHN and France LiDAR HD. It reuses the existing surface reader's request/GeoTIFF decoding, with a separate versioned `dtm_grid` cache and **bilinear** terrain reprojection (never the DSM highest-value rule). It reads 60 m around the requested crop to recognise enclosed survey holes; missing cells within 30 m of measured ground are filled in enclosed components and within 60 m in open land gaps whose coarse reference is above 0.3 m. Cached masks keep interpolation from borrowing previously interpolated values; the report gives measured, interpolated and fallback fractions of the read raster. Reference sea cells stay missing and use the ordinary DEM fallback; the fallback covers the entire padded raster, avoiding edge extrapolation when classifying land. The report counts both interpolation passes explicitly. `dem.fetch_dem(auto)` tries the applicable terrain providers before IGN/Terrarium; explicit preferences preserve their old behaviour. The source grid is coarsened when necessary to the existing mesh budget, and the report records effective sampling and any interpolation. `area_check` exposes the same candidate source metadata through UI, REST and MCP without downloading a raster. A stage-version bump invalidates gathered heights derived from the old ground; historical builds remain available for A/B comparison.

- **params.py**: `ModelParams`, the one validated object that decides a model (centre, ground size, shape, print size, base, exaggerations, resolution, layers, worldwide fills (`landcover`, `dsm_heights`, `tree_source`, `buildings_source`), fields and crop rows, squares, roads, contours, frame, its style and plaque, label, highlighted place and route, nozzle and `thicken`, tiles, pins, framed panels and `tile_cuts`, colour mode, elevation source), the three presets and the derived values (scale, model size, cell size on the ground, road rank by scale, automatic contour interval, tile size). `MAX_CELLS` (3 million) bounds the terrain grid.
- **geo.py**: a local projection around the centre with the WGS84 radii of curvature (error far below a millimetre of model for areas of tens of km); `Area` (centre + metres) and its boxes.
- **sources/http.py**: `Fetcher`, an httpx client with an on-disk cache (`data/cache/<bucket>/<sha>`) keyed by the request: Terrarium tiles and IGN rasters forever, Overpass answers 30 days, place searches 90 days, cadastre answers 120 days, the footprint manifest 14 days and its files 180 days, the surface-model STAC catalogue one day. `cached`/`store` give direct access to a bucket (used for the arrays and the map-data stage below).
- **sources/dem.py**: `DemGrid` (raster + pixel mapping, mercator or lon/lat, bilinear sampling) and `Composite` (fills the primary's no-data from a fallback). IGN: WCS 2.0.1 `GetCoverage` of `Elevacion4258_{5,25,200}`, split in requests of at most 1800 px a side, mosaicked, no-data below −1000; coverage chosen as the coarsest grid not coarser than the model cell (and at most 5400 px a side). Terrarium: the zoom whose pixel is just finer than the model cell, at most 144 tiles.
- **sources/osm.py**: one Overpass query (`out geom`) for buildings and building parts, water areas (with pools and fountains), waterways, weirs and dams, highways, railways, green areas, trees and rows of trees, bare rock, scree and glaciers; servers raced (a new one every 6 s until one answers, three rounds). `parse` projects everything to metres, stitches multipolygon members into rings (outer united, inner cut), skips building ways that belong to a building relation (and `type=building` relations), reads heights (`height`, `building:height`, levels + half the roof levels, or the type), roofs (`roof:shape`, `roof:height`/`roof:levels`, `roof:orientation`), road widths (`width`, lanes × 3.3 m or the class), bridges and tunnels (railways too), splits the green into woods and parks, drops outlines whose 3D parts cover 60 % of them, and returns `Features`.
- **sources/catastro.py**: the Spanish cadastre's INSPIRE buildings WFS (`BU.BUILDINGPART`, GML 3.2 in EPSG:4326, no key): the area is asked in cells of 0.005° (a cell that returns the 5,000-part limit is split in four), four at a time, cached 120 days; `parse` reads footprints (with courtyards) and `numberOfFloorsAboveGround`; `to_buildings` turns parts into `Building`s (floors × 3 m + 1.2 m; small low parts are houses), `merge` keeps OpenStreetMap's 3D parts, mapped roofs and landmark buildings and drops the cadastre parts under them.
- **sources/aerial.py**: `cover` reads a mountain's ground from the photo at a few metres a pixel (excess green → vegetation, dark green → woods, brownish → dry grass, bright neutral grey → bare stone; shadows are none of them), used by `build` with `ground_from_photo` (rock = bare stone on slopes over 10°, unclear pixels on slopes over the set angle, any cliff over 60°; woods add tree scatter). The PNOA orthophoto WMS of the IGN (`OI.OrthoimageCoverage`, JPEG, tiles of 2000 px at about 0.5 m/px, at most 64 tiles, cached a year); `canopy` = excess-green index (smoothed) above 0.03 and smoothed brightness below 112, opened; `trees` = crowns at the peaks of the distance transform of the canopy (thinned so they do not pile up), crown size from that distance.
- **sources/canopy.py**: trees anywhere from the global canopy height map of Meta and the World Resources Institute (Cloud-Optimised GeoTIFFs in web mercator on AWS, one per zoom-9 quadkey, about 1.2 m a pixel, vegetation height in metres, no key). `fetch` reads only the window over the area through GDAL range requests (rasterio), at most 4,200 px a side (coarser past that, keeping the tallest) and caches it as a compressed array (`canopy` bucket). `trees` = local maxima of the smoothed heights, at least 3 m tall, thinned so crowns do not pile up (minimum spacing from the model's smallest tree), crown size from the height; at most 60,000 trees. `build` uses it when `trees`, `tree_source` auto and the scale is finer than about 1:15.000, then adds the aerial photo's trees in Spain and the mapped ones.
- **sources/landcover.py**: ESA WorldCover 2021 (10 m, eleven classes, 3 × 3 degree Cloud-Optimised GeoTIFFs in lon/lat on AWS, no key). `fetch` reads the window over the area (at most 4,000 px a side) and caches it (`landcover` bucket); `area` turns classes into a smoothed shapely area in metres. The groups are woods, green (trees, scrub, grass, wetland, mangrove, moss), fields (cropland), rock (bare), snow and water.
- **sources/msbuildings.py**: Microsoft's Global ML Building Footprints (footprints traced from imagery, ODbL, with a height for some). A manifest (`dataset-links.csv`, cached 14 days) lists gzip files of one GeoJSON feature per line per country and zoom-9 quadkey; only the files over the area are downloaded (`msbuildings` bucket, at most 400 MB together) and only the features inside it are parsed. `to_buildings` makes `Building`s (a house when small, 5.5 m or 8 m when the footprint has no height) and `fill` adds the footprints that overlap an OpenStreetMap building by less than a third of their area; an OpenStreetMap building without a height takes the height of the footprint over it.
- **sources/openbuildings.py**: Google Open Buildings 2.5D Temporal (public bucket `open-buildings-temporal-data`): the GCS listing of `v1/manifests/` (cached a month) gives the manifests per S2 cell, UTM zone and year; the newest year's tiles over the area (12.5 km, 0.5 m COGs with building presence and height) are read in blocks of 1.5 km at 1 m, presence smoothed and thresholded at 0.5, touching roofs split by their bright cores, blobs under 12 m² dropped, near-rectangles squared, height the median inside; returned in the same shape as the footprints (`msbuildings.to_buildings`/`fill` reuse it). Only where `covers` (Africa, South and South-East Asia, Latin America).
- **sources/dsm.py**: real roof and tower heights from open national laser surface models, without a key: WCS 2.0.1 / 1.0.0 and WMS services (Netherlands AHN4, France IGN LiDAR HD, England Environment Agency, North Rhine-Westphalia, Norway, Poland, Flanders) and swisstopo's swissSURFACE3D Cloud-Optimised GeoTIFFs (found through its STAC catalogue). Each `Provider` has a rough country outline; `providers_for` lists the ones covering the area, regional first (Spain: Navarre IDENA, Gipuzkoa b5m, Cantabria, then the national PNOA-LiDAR model of `wcs-mds.idee.es`, 0.5 m where flown and 2.5 m elsewhere); a keyed provider (Denmark, the `dataforsyningen_token` setting) is skipped without its key; `fetch` falls back to the next one when a service fails. `fetch` asks the service in its own coordinate system in tiles below its size limit (raw answers in the `dsm` bucket), puts them on one lon/lat grid of 1 m (2 m over 9 km²), keeping the highest value where a coarse pixel covers several fine ones, and caches the grid (`dsm_grid` bucket). `trees` finds the crowns the laser sees (surface model minus a ground of minima over 24 m, buildings masked, lone spikes out but a fir's tip kept, local tops at least 3 m tall whose surroundings are rough), with `crown_widths` (where the heights fall under a quarter of the top, 12 directions) and `crown_kinds` (a conifer when a quarter of the way out the crown is already a fifth lower), stored as `Features.laser_trees` and drawn before the canopy map; `roof_fit` fits the laser heights of a footprint to a gabled roof (ridge along or across), a hipped one or a flat one in the frame of its smallest rectangle (least squares on the distance to the walls; slope 8°–61°, fit under 0.8 m and 60 % of the flat one's); `roof_tiers` turns a big roof that is not pitched into levels (opening 6 m, median 7 m, steps of 1.5 m or 8 %, levels 8 m wide at least, simplified 2 m, the three biggest); `building_heights` gives, for each footprint, a roof height (90th percentile of the height above ground inside it), a top height and the towers (connected parts more than 6 m over the roof and at least 6 m² wide); the ground is taken from a ring around the footprint unless the model is already heights above ground (France offers that).
- **sources/fake.py**: the invented town for `GULLIVER_FAKE=1` and the tests, in the Overpass format, laid out around any centre.
- **mesh/build.py**: `build(params, sources)` → `BuildResult`. Steps: elevation grid (`resolution_mm` cells; on radar elevation `_fill_pits` first fills, by priority flood from the border, the closed hollows at least 4 m deep and under 6 ha — shore and void artefacts —; then smoothed by about 0.45 × source resolution / cell), sea (≤ 0.3 m and connected to the border; its outline traced from the elevation with contourpy's marching squares on a grid padded 1 mm past the border), Z in mm (`base + (elev − ref) × scale × z_exag`, the exaggeration lowered when the relief would pass `max_relief_ratio` of the longest side), map data, gathered as one `Features` and kept in the `stages` cache for 7 days under a key (`_stage_key`) made of the area, the sources and the layers asked for and the scale gates that decide whether the surface model and the parcels are fetched, so a rebuild with other print settings skips the whole stage (it is not stored when a source failed): OpenStreetMap, the cadastre's buildings (Spain), the worldwide footprints where the cadastre is not used (`msbuildings.fill`), the land cover (`_apply_landcover`: woods, grass and scrub outside towns, crops, rock on mountains, snow, lakes nobody traced), the cadastre's farm parcels (Spain) and the laser surface heights (`_apply_dsm`: buildings drawn only as outlines take their roof height — a known height, such as the cadastre's floors, is kept only when the laser roof is under 0.6 × it − 2 m —, the fitted roof shape (`roof_source` laser) or the levels (`roof_tiers`, drawn by `_tiered` as stacked prisms), only where enough of the model's own cells (not the grid's) fall on the roof, each tower or spire becomes a part standing on the roof), then roads buffered by their widths and closed (gaps under 0.36 mm filled, islands under 0.8 mm² filled), rails as their own outline, paths (paths below the drawn road rank: inside the green in a town, everywhere with `meadows`; cut out of the snow, rock and green layers), contour lines traced as lines (0.5 mm wide); with `thicken`, road, rail and path outlines, and later each building footprint, are widened to two nozzle passes (`printability.thicken`); the route (`track.parse` → `to_metres`) buffered by its width, kept off the water; the farm fields (`fields.parcels`: farmland and crops minus woods, parks, water, roads, rails and the route, cut along the edges of the mapped fields, the cadastre's parcels, roads, paths, rails, waterways and hedges, each parcel shrunk so 0.45 mm of ground stays between neighbours; `fields.all_rows`: crop rows along the long side of each parcel, closer in vineyards and orchards, none in meadows). The terrain solid (`solids.heightfield`, simplified with `Manifold.simplify` to 0.02 mm, which removes about four fifths of the triangles of flat ground and makes the colour cuts of the Parts step about three times faster) is then raised (raised roads, railway beds, the route ribbon: the outline's prism ∩ a copy of the terrain moved up and shrunk 0.05 mm) and cut (engraved roads, contours, park paths in one colour, crop rows, and in one colour the gaps between fields: the outline's prism − a copy moved down and grown 0.05 mm), so every edge follows the vector outline. Tunnel mouths are cut in the same pass: at each end of a tunnel road or railway at least 1 mm inside the model where the ground 1.2 mm into the tunnel stands 0.4 mm over a mouth at least 0.6 mm high, a prism from the road floor along the first 1.2 mm of the tunnel (and 0.3 mm out) is taken out of the hill. Water (`_water_levels`: a level grid, body by body: the lowest bare ground of the body within 150 m of ground, smoothed along the water and held under the ground around each point; a body whose level varies less than 0.25 mm is flat at its lowest point; the cut is the outline's prism minus the heightfield of level − depth, the water part the prism between that floor and the level heightfield, both on a sub-grid padded 1.5 mm past the outline; the sea one prism to a flat floor), bridges over water (`detail.arch_bridge`: a 0.5 mm deck on piers and semicircular arches about 28 m of ground apart, decks that touch share the higher height), bridges off the water (over a street, a railway, a ravine: drawn when the bare-earth elevation dips under them at least 0.35 + 0.15 mm below the deck; a 0.35 mm deck, on arches where there is room, else a plain deck resting on its ends), weirs and dams (a wall across the water from under its floor to 0.25 / 0.6 mm above the level, in the terrain's colour), piers, breakwaters and groynes (`man_made`, the same way: a deck 0.4 / 0.6 / 0.3 mm above the inland level or the sea), shore lines (`water_lines`: 0.25 mm grooves 0.15 mm deep at 0.7 + 0.8·i mm from the shore in water at least 5 mm wide: the outline's band minus the level heightfield lowered 0.15 mm; flat on the sea), buildings (cadastre parts or OSM; small ones kept when their block is big enough; simplified, grown 0.02 mm so neighbours overlap; walls from under the lowest ground point to the eaves and `detail.building_solid` roofs; parts with a `min_height` float on the parts under them), the highlighted place (the building under the point with the parts on it, taken out of the buildings, or a `detail.marker` pin where there is none), trees (`detail.plant` + `detail.trees_solid`: trees known one by one — mapped, then the laser's (`Features.laser_trees`), the canopy map's (not fetched when the laser saw trees), hedges as rows of bushes, then the aerial photo's in Spain, skipping the ones already known — rows, woods and parks scattered when no survey sees the trees; conifers as cones, others as ellipsoid crowns sunk under the ground; conifer kind from OpenStreetMap's `leaf_type`, needle-leaved woods or `detail.conifer_share` by latitude and altitude; with `thicken` no crown is smaller than the nozzle). Colour mode: each layer is the top 0.6 mm of the terrain inside its outline (`cutter = prism − lowered terrain`; `layer = terrain ∩ cutter`, `terrain −= cutter`), in priority roads › rails › plazas › snow › rock › fields › green, each 0.08 mm off the ones before, off the banks and 0.03 mm off building walls; road and rail layers sit 0.03 mm inside their groove or bed walls; trees are their own part (trees − ground − buildings − highlight), and so are the highlight and the route; a one-piece twin (`_single`) follows every later step for the STL. Then the outline shape (intersection with its prism), the frame (`_frame`: a band around the outline in the style `frame_style` — flat, bevel with a chamfer, or step — standing 1 mm over the ground along its edge; the copy used in unions reaches 0.05 mm inside; with `plaque`, the largest strip of the south side (`plaque.plaque_strip`) is filled by `plaque.plaque_layout` and raised, and in colour the frame is cut where it goes; the frame is widened to 14 mm to carry a plaque, and to 8 mm for panels if none was asked), the text under the model (Pillow renders it, `mask_to_polygons` turns the pixels into polygons, mirrored), tiles (intersection with each tile's box, or with the polygon from `seams.street_tiles` when `tile_cuts` is streets) with pin holes (horizontal cylinders across each seam, every ~45 mm where the footprint allows; with street seams `_seam_pins` puts them square to the seam, clear of buildings and of the edge) and the tile letter under each tile. Framed panels use the same loop: the model is cut into `panels_x` × `panels_y` boxes, each gets its own `_frame` and plaque (subtitle with `i/n`) and no pins, and print tiles are ignored. At the end the stats are collected (the keys the README lists as the report) and `printability.analyse` runs on the result.
- **mesh/detail.py**: roofs (frame of the smallest rectangle around the footprint; gabled/hipped/skillion as `CrossSection.extrude` with a top scale, pyramids and cones to a point, domes and barrels from a scaled sphere or cylinder, clipped to the footprint when it is not convex), trees (jittered hexagonal scatter, rows sampled along lines, ellipsoid crowns), arch bridges (a profile in the along/up plane — deck, piers, arches — extruded across the road per straight segment), snow line and slope.
- **mesh/fields.py**: 2D only, in millimetres. `parcels` cuts the farmland mask along the edge lines (a hair of ground, `gap_mm`, stays between neighbours; crumbs under a minimum area are dropped); `rows` makes parallel strips along the long side of a parcel (`axis_deg` of its minimum rotated rectangle), kept in from its edge, none when the parcel is too narrow; `kind_at` finds the mapped land use of a parcel; `all_rows` does it for every parcel (vineyards, orchards and nurseries at 0.7 of the spacing, meadows none, L-shaped or ring-shaped leftovers skipped unless the photo gives a direction); `angles` (from `aerial.row_angles`: the structure tensor of the orthophoto's brightness summed inside each parcel shrunk by 3 px, rows across the main gradient when its coherence is at least 0.2; photo at 1 m/px, coarser past 16 Mpx) overrides the long side per parcel.
- **mesh/plaque.py**: the flat artwork of the plaque in millimetres: the title, a line with coordinates and scale, a north arrow and a scale bar (`north_arrow`, `scale_bar`, `plaque_layout`). It never draws text smaller than the size whose bold stems reach the minimum stroke of the nozzle: when the strip is too small the bar goes first, then the subtitle, and a title that still does not fit is shortened with an ellipsis. `plaque_strip` finds the largest rectangle of the frame along one side, `place` moves the artwork onto it.
- **mesh/seams.py**: `street_tiles` cuts the box into `nx` × `ny` tiles whose seams wander inside a narrow band around the straight line (at most 18 % of the tile size, 15 mm) along the cheapest path on a cost grid (streets cheap, buildings expensive; Dijkstra on a sparse graph), and the tiles are rebuilt as an exact partition of the box. With no buildings and no roads, or if the construction cannot be validated, the straight tiles come back.
- **printability.py**: `analyse(result, nozzle_mm)` reads a finished `BuildResult` and returns the `printability` entry of the report: per part the bodies, the smallest and the tiny ones (volume under four cubic nozzle widths), buildings thinner than two nozzle passes, the thin share of the road, rail, path and green layers (`thin_regions`: the outline minus its opening), tree crowns thinner than two passes, a `score` 0-100, a `verdict` (`ok` from 80, `review` from 55, else `risky`) and `advice` in Spanish that names the parameter to change, with `fixes` (parameter, current value, proposed value: `tree_size_mm`, `resolution_mm`, `track_width_mm`, `min_road_mm`, `min_path_mm`, `base_mm`, `water_depth_mm`, and `thicken` turned on when streets, rails or paths are thin) for `Services.advice` to preview and apply. `thicken` widens what is thinner than a given width; `build` calls it with `thicken` on.
- **track.py**: reads the text of a GPX, KML or GeoJSON route (the format is recognised from the content), drops points outside the globe and repeated ones, `to_metres` projects the lines around the map centre, and `centre_and_size` suggests the centre and ground size (at least 300 m) that frame the whole route with a 15 % margin. `agent_tools._around_track` uses it so a `track` without `lon`/`lat` frames the model; the form's button does the same.
- **media.py**: stores the product photo (`photo.png`) and the turntable video (`turntable.webm`) that the browser renders in the 3D viewer (`client/src/components/ProductShot.jsx`), in the folder of the model's current build: `save_media` checks the content type, the PNG or EBML magic bytes and the 60 MB limit; `list_media` lists what is there. Routes in `api/ui.py`: `POST /api/models/{id}/media/{photo|video}` (raw body), `GET /api/models/{id}/media` and `GET /api/models/{id}/media/{name}`.
- **mesh/solids.py**: the heightfield solid (top grid with alternating diagonals, four walls, a fan bottom), prisms from shapely polygons (`CrossSection`, even-odd), outline shapes, text, tiles, pins, `mesh_arrays` (copies manifold's arrays, welds vertices that fall on the same float32 position and drops collapsed faces; the last 16 results are kept), `open_edges`, `unpinch` (a 7-sided rod 0.03 mm across subtracted along every edge shared by four faces: two bodies of one part touching along a line) and `clean` (unpinch, then bodies of no volume dropped, found as connected components of the welded mesh with scipy — manifold's own `decompose` is slow on parts with thousands of bodies).
- **worker.py**: `run_isolated(job, args)` starts a spawned process (`child_main`) that builds and writes the files with its own `Sources`, relays progress through a queue and returns the report; cancel terminates it. Used by the app (`Config.isolate`, on unless in demo mode or `GULLIVER_ISOLATE=0`); the tests build in process.
- **mesh/export.py**: binary STL; 3MF (core spec: `basematerials` with the part colours, one mesh object per part, an assembly object of components, metadata, thumbnail; vertices with six decimals, or nine significant digits when six would merge two of them); GLB through trimesh (nodes `<tile>__<part>`, simplified past 1.2 M triangles); the top-view thumbnail (layers, rock, snow, rails and paths on the ground colour, all multiplied by the hillshade so the relief shows through them; then trees + buildings with a shadow, cut to the outline).
- **outputs.py**: writes a build folder (`data/projects/<id>/builds/<build>/`): `<slug>.stl`, `<slug>.3mf`, `tiles/<slug>-<id>.{stl,3mf}`, `<slug>-tiles.zip` (with `LEEME.txt`), `preview.glb`, `thumb.png` and `report.json` (stats, parts, tiles with sizes, fit to the bed and their own check, file sizes, notes, attribution; the stats include `trees_from`, `conifers`, `footprints_added`, `landcover`, `dsm_buildings`, `fields`, `plazas`, `highlight`, `track`, `plaque`, `tile_cuts` and `printability`). The photo and video of `media.py` live in the same folder.
- **projects.py**: `project.json` per model (params, status, the current build and its summary, the builds kept and their `history`: id, time, the parameters each was made with — also in the build's `params.json` — and its summary with the printability score), pruning to `keep_builds`; `builds`, `use_build` (a kept build current again, with its parameters) and `build_folder`; files are served from the current build or a kept one (`build_id`), only by a relative name.
- **jobs.py**: one worker thread (the build itself in `worker.py`'s child process); `Job.report(fraction, step)` feeds the progress and the log; cancel is checked between steps; `jobs.json` keeps the last 200 (running ones become `lost` after a restart).
- **services.py**: glue for the tools and routes: `make_params` (defaults + preset + values, or a patch over a stored model), `area_check` (with `ModelParams.effective_frame_mm`: the plaque's 14 mm and the panels' 8 mm; for framed panels it suggests panels with `suggest_panels`), `builds`, `compare_builds` (parameters that differ, size, weight, triangles, buildings, tiles, score, parts added or gone), `use_build`, `advice` (the printability `fixes` previewed with two `area_check`s, or applied: new parameters and a rebuild that keeps the build before it), the settings with secrets masked (`public_settings`, `source_keys` for the sources and the build process), create/update/build/delete, files, export to a folder, Vulcan through `family.call`, cache, and the family events (`gulliver.model.created`, `gulliver.model.ready`, `gulliver.export.done`).

Data folder (`data/`): `settings.json`, `jobs.json`, `projects/<id>/project.json`, `projects/<id>/builds/<build>/…`, `cache/{terrarium,ign,overpass,nominatim,catastro,aerial,canopy,landcover,msbuildings,dsm,dsm_grid,stages}`, `mcp-token`, `logs/`.

Interface (`client/`, React + Vite + Tailwind, the shared Hoard theme with the `gulliver` palette): `#/` library, `#/nueva` (and `#/nueva?edit=<id>`) map + parameters with the live `area_check`, `#/m/<id>` the 3D viewer (three.js, GLTFLoader, OrbitControls; Z-up model rotated into three's Y-up) and the report, `#/ajustes` settings. `copilot-context.js` is what the shared Faustus panel reads.

Colour palettes (`mesh/build.py`, `PALETTES`): part → colour for `classic`, `sand`, `grey`, `white` and `alpine`; `BuildResult.colors` carries the chosen one to the 3MF (`basematerials`), the GLB materials, the thumbnail and `report.json`. Towers (`tall_buildings_m`): the building solids at least that tall are united as `towers`; the `buildings` part is the rest (`buildings − towers`), so the two never share volume. Snow (`detail.snow_mask`): above the line where the slope is under 40°, and within max(60 m, 0.35 × (top − line)) below it where the ground falls towards the pole and the slope is under 35°; traced from the smoothed mask.
